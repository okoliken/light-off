# Story direction (October 2026, v3: see story-bible.md for the missions)

This replaces every earlier framing: no kid, no Mama, no Tobi, no student. It's the creator's direction, tidied up.

## Bolaji
- **An adult trying to live in Lagos.** He rents a single room ("face-me-I-face-you") and the rent is due every week. Nobody looks after him.
- **By day: a delivery rider without a bike.** He works for a dispatch company (SwiftDrop Dispatch). He collects the day's parcels at the office and gets them to customers on time, paying for danfos, kekes and okadas out of his own pocket, or skating.
  - He's paid **weekly, per delivery**, not a salary, so performance is everything.
  - Customers in Lagos don't wait. Late parcels mean angry calls and bad reviews.
  - The other riders at the office are competing with him, and some are better.
  - **He can lose the job,** and he can apply again and get it back. Either way he has to survive and pay rent.
- **The daytime pull:** pickpockets, phone snatchers, "one chance" buses. He knows the tricks and can step in, but every distraction runs the parcel clock down.
- **By night: the boy in black.** A different person, free to roam, hiding, investigating and taking jobs apart.

## The enemy: the General
- A powerful man with a military past, highly trained in close combat, who can beat anyone in a straight fight.
- He runs an army of **boys in black**: everywhere, armed with AK-47s and big knives, sent out by day and night to cause havoc for him.
- Some of the police are on his payroll: old soldiers and friends from his army days.
- **The police and Bolaji:** at first the police aren't against him. But the General's boys wear black, and so does he, so the police assume he's one of them. That means **a lot of police chases.**

## How it plays
- **Days:** a checklist of deliveries (where, to whom, by when), the pressure of the clock, reviews and pay, rent, and street trouble that tempts you off course. Along the way Bolaji picks up **leads** about the boys in black.
- **Nights:** investigations, stopping the General's operations, tracing where the boys come from. Lots of fighting, lots of running from the police.
- **The end of the story:** find out who the General is, find the illegal thing the police don't know about, and use it to expose and stop him.
- **Saving:** the game always continues exactly where you were (position, time, job, money), even after closing the browser.

## Modes and missions
The story, the two modes and the ten missions now live in **docs/story-bible.md** (fresh start, 2026-10-07).

- **Missions:** ten missions in order. Each starts at its yellow marker (F) at the right time in the right clothes (night: the suit; day: SwiftDrop work clothes), ends with MISSION COMPLETE, and nothing else happens until you start the next.

## Character pass (live)
- **Grown man, not a kid.** By day: SwiftDrop shirt, cap and trousers. **No board by day.** The board is only for the boy in black.
- **Background (light):** he grew up on the streets around Ojuelegba. **Coach Ayo**, an old gymnast, trained the street kids on the empty track at the National Stadium. He's still there (6 AM to 7 PM) and teaches moves for a fee and an hour of training:
  - wire riding;
  - breakfall (no fall damage);
  - long wall-run;
  - vanishing (longer smoke);
  - big cat leap.
- **The escape kit:**
  - **1 pepper bomb:** blinds a group.
  - **2 ash cloud:** police can't see into it.
  - **3 nail plank:** a police car that runs over it is finished.
  - **Ride the NEPA wires (E):** slide pole to pole along the street.
  - **Bolekaja (E):** hang off the back of a danfo, then Space to climb onto the roof and ride it.
  - Pull a transformer fuse for a blackout, as before.
  - Refills: Mama Chi Provisions (pepper, ash) and Bright Future Electricals (nails) in the malls.
